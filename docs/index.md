# Welcome to Civic AI in Town
**Civic AI** is artificial intelligence designed to answer directly to the people it affects, rather than acting as a universal, top-down governing authority. Defined by Taiwan's former Digital Minister and cyber ambassador Audrey Tang, Civic AI shifts the focus of technology from automating labor or centralizing control to building small, community-owned systems that people can inspect, correct, and switch off. 

## Core Concepts of Civic AI
**Local Accountability**: Instead of one massive cloud model trying to rule everyone, Civic AI uses many small, bounded systems deployed at a community scale.

**The "Kami"**: Tang uses the term Kami (a spirit of place or local knowledge artifact) to describe these specialized, local stewards that handle specific community tasks and are easily replaceable when needs change.

**[The 6-Pack of Care](https://civic.ai)**: Grounded in political philosopher Joan Tronto's work on the ethics of care, this framework relies on six design principles: attentiveness, responsibility, competence, responsiveness, solidarity, and symbiosis.

**Broad Listening and Social Translation**: Rather than fueling polarization like traditional social media, Civic AI tools (such as Pol.is or structured deliberation agents) summarize large groups of diverse inputs to help citizens find common ground and shared agreements. 

## The Core Details of Tang's FOSS AI Framework
Audrey Tang’s approach to Open-Source Freedoms (FOSS) in Artificial Intelligence reframes the original four foundational software freedoms—originally established by the free software movement—as crucial civic muscles needed to govern AI democratically. [1](https://www.stairjournal.com/oped/2026/6/27/civic-ai-and-the-muscles-of-care-reflections-from-ambassador-audrey-tang), [2](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/article/digest-week-4-trinity-term-2026), [3] (https://english.cw.com.tw/article/article.action?id=3795)
Rather than viewing AI as an unstoppable force or a corporate black box, Tang argues that treating AI through a FOSS framework allows local communities to reclaim agency from large tech corporations. [1](https://civic.ai/software-freedom-as-civic-care/), [2] (https://blog.tib.eu/2026/06/19/what-do-we-mean-when-we-talk-about-ai-and-open-knowledge/)



### Rereading the Four Freedoms as Civic Muscles
Tang maps the classic four software freedoms directly onto how societies should interact with AI systems: [1](https://www.stairjournal.com/oped/2026/6/27/civic-ai-and-the-muscles-of-care-reflections-from-ambassador-audrey-tang), [2](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/article/digest-week-4-trinity-term-2026)

    •    Attentiveness (Freedom to Run): Derived from the freedom to run a program for any purpose. In AI, this means a local school, clinic, or temple must be allowed to deploy an AI tool to attend to its unique community needs without needing explicit permission from corporate tech designers. [1](https://www.stairjournal.com/oped/2026/6/27/civic-ai-and-the-muscles-of-care-reflections-from-ambassador-audrey-tang)
 
    •    Inspectability (Freedom to Study): The freedom to look under the hood. Instead of being trapped in proprietary "closed stacks," code, parameters, and model workflows must be open to audit so citizens can see how a system works. [1](https://www.moesif.com/blog/technical/api-development/Open-Source-AI/), [2](https://www.hbs.edu/bigs/taiwans-digital-revolution-audrey-tang), [3](https://civic.ai/software-freedom-as-civic-care/)
      •    Repairability (Freedom to Modify): The freedom to adapt the system. If an AI behaves with bias or breaks within a local context, users must have the right to fork the code, fine-tune the weights, and fix it directly rather than waiting on a corporate customer service ticket. [1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-BUZViHK4o&t=125), [2](https://www.moesif.com/blog/technical/api-development/Open-Source-AI/), [3](https://civic.ai/software-freedom-as-civic-care/)
    
    •    Solidarity (Freedom to Share): The freedom to redistribute copies. This permits communities to share optimized, locally safe versions of AI models with other communities to scale collective well-being. [1](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/law-in-tech/2024/free-and-open-source-software-and-ai), [2](https://www.moesif.com/blog/technical/api-development/Open-Source-AI/), [3](https://civic.ai/software-freedom-as-civic-care/)

### Escaping the Corporate "Hamster Wheel"
Tang contrasts open-source AI with the experience of using a proprietary, closed AI stack. Tang compares closed AI interfaces to a beautiful, fast-rotating hamster wheel: users feel like they are moving, but they cannot steer the system, they cannot write to an upstream repository, and they have no power to fix it when it breaks. FOSS freedoms allow communities to break out of this developer-controlled loop and pull AI tools entirely into local, accountable community structures. [1](https://civic.ai/software-freedom-as-civic-care/)

### Being a "Good Enough Ancestor"
Tang frames code forkability and open infrastructure as an ethical obligation to future generations. By ensuring AI architectures remain open, digital infrastructure functions similarly to ancient libraries—artifacts that remain open for the next reader to critique, comment on, and build upon without central enclosure. [1](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/article/digest-week-4-trinity-term-2026)
